# X3Lang round 23 — 2026-09-18 — coordinator-facing settlement plan (spec item 20)

Landed on `master` as `7a4d1184f` (base `40ccd1517`). Spec build-order item 20.

## What the plan says now

```
PARALLEL_PLAN …;settle=0:evm+svm:-:coordinated|1:evm:-:local
```

Per wave: the domains it spans, the proof inputs its bridges need and do not
carry (named with the chain each is owed on), and whether the VM's own rollback
can undo it. A wave confined to one domain is `local`; a wave spanning domains is
`coordinated`, because its effects on the other domain are already out of this
VM's hands. Both verifiers check the section is coherent and the VM records it;
an absent or misaligned record is refused rather than read as "no obligations".

## Three defects found, all in code I wrote in round 21

1. **Bridging inside a parallel leg was impossible.** `timeout` was parsed only
   in intent bodies, so a leg could not declare its expiry, and the verdict on a
   bridging leg was "no refund path" — correct, and unanswerable by the program.
2. **A leg could not declare the program's replay-protection nonce.** The guard
   lives in a body and a leg is a body; a leg's nonce now reaches the program
   metadata, and two legs disagreeing are refused rather than ordered.
3. **The race check was wrong in both directions.** It compared writes across the
   whole block, so two legs in *different* waves that both write the same asset
   were called a race even though the dependency between them was what made them
   safe. And the read/write prune I added in round 21 (`an asset a leg both reads
   and writes is internal, so drop the read`) erased real dependencies: a leg
   that bridges another leg's output also carries a refund path on that asset,
   the refund path lowers to a `Release`, and pruning the read against that write
   left the two legs looking unordered.

The prune is gone. A leg that writes what another produces now gets both the edge
and the overlap; the overlap check sees the ordering and stays quiet. The cost is
the other direction — a leg that locks an asset it also consumes now depends on
whoever produces it, serialising two legs that might have run together. Ordering
too much is a lost opportunity; ordering too little is a race.

None of the three was reachable before this round, because no test and no example
had ever bridged inside a parallel leg. Writing the first realistic fixture found
all three.

## Fixtures were wrong three more times

- Both legs swapping *into* the same asset is a genuine race; the compiler was
  right and my fixture was wrong.
- `transfer_proof` on its own line is not part of the bridge step and is silently
  ignored. The plan still reported the obligation, so the program was told — but a
  stray option line doing nothing is its own papercut (TICKET-037).
- `bridge` without `amount` defaults to zero and the IR refuses it with a message
  that names the fix.
