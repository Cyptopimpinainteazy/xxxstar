## The classification's first pass, and where it pointed

TICKET-094 is 551 float sites repo-wide, and the criterion is which of them compute a value
that becomes a **balance, a reward, a slash, or a settlement amount**. Two crates whose names
say an amount is computed, read end to end:

**`x3-gpu-validator-swarm/src/payment.rs` — a payment path, and it had the defect.**

```rust
.checked_mul((rate.verification_bonus * 1000.0) as u128).and_then(|r| (r / 1000).try_into().ok())
```

A cast of a float product truncates. `1.001` is not representable in binary — it is
`1.0009999999999999…` — so `1.001 * 1000.0` is `1000.9999999999999` and the cast gives
`1000`. **The bonus was applied as `1.000` and the provider was paid one part in a thousand
short.**

Measured over `0.000..2.000`:

```
rates where (x * 1000.0) as u128 != x*1000 exactly: 12 of 2001
first ten: (1.001, 1000, 1001) (1.003, 1002, 1003) (1.005, 1004, 1005) (1.007, 1006, 1007) …
```

Twelve of two thousand and one, and every one is a rate an operator could name. This is
PHASE 43's prohibition in its most concrete form: not a style question about floats, but **an
amount of money that depends on whether a decimal is representable in binary**.

**`x3-staking-analytics` — analytics, and that is a classification rather than a
conversion.** Its `estimate_reward` is called only inside its own crate, the crate's only
dependent is `x3-rpc` (a query surface), and nothing in a settlement or balance path reads
those amounts. `validator_stats.rs`'s `annual_backing_reward`, `annual_commission_from_backing`
and `nominator_net_reward` return amounts and are consumed by nothing but each other. So the
floats there are *estimates returned to a query surface*, and the honest answer is to say so
and move on — recorded so the next pass does not re-open it.

(One detail worth noting for that crate's own audit rather than this one: `slash_tracker.rs`
reads `Utc::now()`. In a *score* that is a statistic; had it reached a decision, PHASE 42
would have something to say about a wall clock in a pure function.)

## The fix

The rates are integers in thousandths: `verification_bonus_per_mille: 1_500` is `1.5×`. The
multiplier is then exact and the truncation is gone. `PaymentAmount` is a `u64` and the rates
are of the same kind, which is what they were always trying to be.

`a_rate_that_binary_cannot_hold_is_not_lost` pins it: a `1.001×` bonus on a base of 1000 must
pay 1001. **Verified both ways** — with the old float expression the test fails (`left: 1000`
against `right: 1001`) and with the integer form it passes.

## A process note, because it nearly cost the evidence

The first attempt to simulate the old behaviour substituted
`f64::from(rate.verification_bonus_per_mille)` — and **`f64::from(u64)` does not exist**, so
the crate did not compile. My check grepped the output for `^test result|left|right|panicked`
and found nothing — which is exactly what a *passing* run with a different filter would look
like, and could have been read as "the test does not catch it".

**A simulation that does not compile produces no test result, and an absence is not a
verdict.** The second attempt used `as f64` and captured the whole output, which is where the
`left: 1000` came from.
