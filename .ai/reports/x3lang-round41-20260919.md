# X3Lang round 41 — 2026-09-19 — a trading deadline is a duration (TICKET-048)

Landed on `master` as `78f6bb566` (base `7ff72c3e1`).

## One language, two answers

`parse_deadline_expr` accepted `deadline: N blocks` and rejected any other unit:

    Trading Core v1 deadline currently requires 'blocks'; 'seconds' is not supported

while the timeout clauses accepted `180s`, `40m`, `2h`. So a program could write a
timeout in minutes and not a deadline in seconds, and the language had two answers
about what a duration is. The deadline now goes through the same
`parse_duration_expr` and the same conversion (`expression_to_blocks`), so
`deadline: 2h` and `timeout 2h` are the same 1200 blocks at the block time named
once in `SECONDS_PER_BLOCK` — and a fraction of a block is refused rather than
truncated, because the lowering no longer demands an integer literal.

The unit can also be written as its own word (`30 seconds`, `2 hours`), which the
timeout clauses now accept too; `blocks` stays a count of blocks. `literal_u64` in
the trading lowering had no other caller and is gone rather than left as dead code.

## What this round did not do

The timeout/per-deadline machinery still has one shape left worth a ticket: the
*ordering* invariant exists for `atomic swap` (source outlasts destination) and
nothing states the equivalent for a trading policy whose deadline must outlast the
settlement path it depends on. Not filed as a ticket here because I have not
measured whether anything relies on the absence.
