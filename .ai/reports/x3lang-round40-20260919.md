# X3Lang round 40 — 2026-09-19 — one slippage literal, one unit (TICKET-054)

Landed on `master` as `7ff72c3e1` (base `d40463f74`).

## Three readers, one quantity

| reader | read `<= 50` as |
|---|---|
| the mainnet ceiling (`extract_slippage_bps`) | 50 bps (but `5.25` as 525 bps, i.e. a *percent*) |
| the risk scorer (`risk.rs`) | 5000 bps, multiplying a bare number by 100 as a whole percent |
| the strategy profile check (`strategy.rs`) | 50, compared directly against `max_slippage_bps` |

So the corpus's most common bound — `require slippage <= 50`, which is 0.5% and the
tightest thing in most of the examples — was reported as

```
slippage_risk        40
· slippage risk: high slippage (5000bps / 50.00%)
```

by `x3c score`, and `<= 120` as 120%. The mainnet gate was internally inconsistent
as well: a bare `50` was 50 bps and a written `5.25` was 525 bps.

## The rule

One rule, in one place, read by all four readers:

- a **bare number is basis points** (`<= 50` is 0.5%),
- a **percent literal is a percentage** (`<= 0.5%` is 50 bps),
- and the two agree where they name one bound — which is the property that makes
  TICKET-052's guard-versus-policy comparison meaningful rather than accidental,
- a fraction of a basis point (`<= 50.5`, `<= 0.005%`) is **refused** rather than
  rounded: `None`, so a check that needs a number says so instead of comparing a
  number nobody wrote,
- a fractional part of zero is not a fraction (`50.0` is 50 bps).

Basis points is the reading two of the three already used, and the one the
declarations are named for (`max_slippage_bps`).

## What this round did not do

The `risk_policy.max_slippage` *field* is still a bare `u64` with zero meaning
"unstated" (TICKET-052's note). Making it `Option<u64>` would let a policy of zero
be stated rather than inferred, which matters once a policy is compared against
guards — recorded, not done, because it is an AST change with readers in the risk
scorer and the linter.
