## The duplication, and what the ticket asked for

PHASE 43 put the amount arithmetic in `x3_lang_common::fixed`. The language does not call
it: it calls `trading_semantic::decimal_to_base_units`, which parses a literal string and
then implements the same rounding direction a second time. TICKET-081 recorded that and
offered two unifications *or* a differential test proving the two agree.

The differential test went first, because it decides how far unification can go and because
it is the safety net for whatever follows.

## The differential test, and why it is the load-bearing part

`compiler/tests/test_amount_conversion_agreement.rs` walks **13 literals × 6 decimals ×
3 directions** through both paths:

```
the_two_conversions_agree_wherever_both_are_defined          ok   234 of 234 compared
an_asset_finer_than_the_fixed_scale_is_the_compilers_alone   ok
```

The first asserts the mapping the ticket named — `Ok(v) ↔ Some(v)` and
`Err(PrecisionLoss) ↔ None` — and **counts the comparisons**, so a table that compared
nothing cannot pass. The second pins the edge below.

Two things the comparison rests on are stated in the test's own doc rather than left to a
reader: the literal is split into whole and fraction *by the test* (that split is the part
one implementation does for itself and the other takes as arguments, so sharing it in the
test is not sharing the behaviour under test), and the 38/18 boundary is asserted rather
than skipped.

## The finding that decides the design: 38 versus 18

The compiler's `MAX_DECIMALS` is **38**. `fixed::MAX_SCALE` is **18**.

```
$ decimal_to_base_units("1.0000000000000000001", 19, Exact)
Ok(10000000000000000001)
$ Decimal::<18>::from_parts(1, "0000000000000000001")
None
```

So there is a domain where only the compiler's path is defined, and **the obvious
unification — delegate to `Decimal` — is impossible**: an 18-scale `u128` mantissa cannot
be widened to 38 without giving up almost all of its range, so this is a property of the
representation rather than an oversight. The ticket's criteria did not anticipate it, and
the boundary is now a fact in the tree instead of a note in a ticket.

## What *can* be shared is the decision

`fixed::apply_rounding` is now the one place the three directions are decided. `div_rounded`
calls it and so does `decimal_to_base_units`. The arithmetic cannot cross the 38/18
boundary; the rule can, and a rule stated twice is a rule that drifts.

`crates/x3-common/tests/fixed_math.rs` gains `the_rounding_decision_is_one_function`,
including `Up` at the top of the range refusing rather than wrapping — the rule every other
`checked_*` in that module follows.

**The refactor is proven behaviour-preserving by the differential test**, which is exactly
what the ticket asked for as its validation: written first, green against two independent
implementations, and still green after one of them became a caller.

## One ordering the refactor had to preserve, and why

The `Exact` refusal stays **before** the arithmetic. Moving the shared rule to the end of
the function would have swapped which error a literal that is both lossy *and* too large
reports — from the loss to the overflow. That is a diagnostic decision rather than an
arithmetic one, and the comment at the call site says so, because the tidier-looking version
is the wrong one.
