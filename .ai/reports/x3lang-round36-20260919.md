# X3Lang round 36 — 2026-09-19 — `canonical_supply` is decided, not recorded (TICKET-027)

Landed on `master` as `5e89e6c7d` (base `99f0ca42a`).

## The claim and the answer

`require canonical_supply USDC` claims the canonical supply of USDC is preserved.
That claim is entirely about the program's *own* operations, so it has a
compile-time answer — and it had neither an answer nor a check: the guard lowered
to a `REQUIRE` the executor treats as `STATIC` (true, unconditionally), and no
other pass read the kind either.

`semantic::verify_canonical_supply(program, ir)` is that answer, run after
lowering in `run_pre_emission_layers` where both halves are in hand:

- the **guards** come from the AST through the shared `require_guards`
  enumeration (the one TICKET-027 introduced so that a guard in a `bridge`,
  `atomic swap`, `strategy` or `proposal` could not escape a check);
- the **operations** come from the flat IR, so a mint inside a nested `atomic`
  block or a choice path counts — walking the AST by hand is what would have
  missed it.

Preserved means **net** zero: minting and burning the same amount has not changed
what is in circulation, and doing either alone has. A contradiction is refused
with the figures — `mints 5 and burns 0` — a guard naming no asset is refused as
unevaluable, and a program that claims nothing may mint freely. Assets compare on
the last dotted segment, so a guard's `USDC` matches an operation's
`solana.USDC` and does not match `solana.SOL`.

## Why this shape, and not a comparison

TICKET-027's criterion is that each guard kind either has a real run-time quantity
at its guard and emits a comparison, **or** is evaluated by the compiler, and
that none may stay an unbacked `STATIC`. `canonical_supply` is the kind whose
quantity is entirely inside the program, so evaluation is the honest choice: a
comparison would need the VM to track supply across a program it can see in full
at compile time.

The ticket now carries a table, one row per kind, saying which is decided and
which is not: three decided (`solver_bond`, `relayer_quorum`, `canonical_supply`),
three partial (`slippage`, `profit`, `refund_path`), seven unbacked (`finality`,
`route_score`, `risk`, `bridge_liquidity`, `nonce`, and the property kinds).
Writing that table is what turned "the other guard kinds" into four concrete
tickets: TICKET-049 (a declaration for each kind that compares against one — and
the six examples that require `route_score >= 85..90` with no declaration
anywhere), TICKET-050 (`risk_policy` silently skips fields it does not know),
TICKET-051 (no nonce registry, so `require nonce unused` is recorded and never
consulted).

## What this round did not do

- The seven unbacked kinds are untouched: each needs a declaration (or a
  registry) the language does not have, and inventing one value at a time is what
  TICKET-049's per-kind acceptance criteria exist to prevent.
- `verify_canonical_supply` reads mint and burn operations, which is what the
  language has. It does not model a *supply audit* where the asset is minted on
  one chain and burned on another with different identifiers — that is a
  cross-chain supply question, and TICKET-049 records it.
