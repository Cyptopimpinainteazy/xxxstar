## 1. Settling the header layout (TICKET-023)

Round 7 ended with a deliberate stop: padding the writer fixed the disassembler
but broke `evm_call_pipeline` and `x3_call_pipeline`, and I would not change one
side of a format on its own. This round enumerated every reader and found the
answer.

There are four readers of the compiler header:

| Reader | Metadata walk |
| --- | --- |
| `emit_x3ir` (writer) | unpadded |
| `vm/executor.rs::first_instruction_pc` | unpadded |
| `compiler/emitter.rs::disassemble` | padded (fixed in round 7) |
| `vm/verifier.rs::skip_compiler_metadata` | padded, and chain_id as u32 + five bytes |

Three of four agree on unpadded, and the executor is the one that actually runs
bytecode, so **the verifier was the outlier**. Padding the writer in round 7 was
the wrong instinct; the failure it caused is what identified the right one.

The verifier's mis-walk was a soundness hole, not just a desync: for any nonce
whose record was 1 or 3 bytes short of a multiple of four it reported the wrong
boundary set, so its payload validation and its "branch target must be
4-aligned" check were applied at offsets that are not instruction starts.
`has_compiler_header` also now uses the executor's stricter rule, so a raw
stream that merely begins with `0x01` is not treated as a compiler stream by one
reader and not the other.

`pad_to_4`'s doc comment claimed the VM verifier requires *every* instruction to
be 4-byte aligned. That claim was false and the verifier acted on it. The
comment now says what is true.

## 2. The trading core could not be validated or executed (new)

With the header settled, `x3c run` on a proof-bearing program worked — and
`examples/trading_core_v1.x3` failed with
`X3_VERIFY_FAILED: InvalidOpcode(176, 1)`. `176` is `0xB0`, `TRADING_BEGIN`, and
`1` is the first instruction offset: the verifier rejected the very first
instruction of every trading program.

Three gaps, all the same shape — the verifier was never updated for the trading
core:

- `valid_opcode` stopped at `0xAB`; trading opcodes are `0xB0..=0xBA`.
- `is_payload_opcode` omitted the same range, so even once accepted, the walk
  advanced a fixed four bytes instead of skipping the real payload.
- `validate_payload_opcode` fell through to `decode_capability_payload`, which
  cannot read trading payloads — they are their own encoding. Trading opcodes now
  decode through the compiler's public `decode_trading_operation`, so a payload
  the verifier accepts has actually been checked against its opcode.

The executor's dispatch had the mirror-image gap: `TRADING_BEGIN..=
TRADING_ASSERT_INVARIANT` stopped one short of `TRADING_BRIDGE` (`0xBA`), which
is emitted by the trading bridge operation and fell through to the
unknown-opcode arm.

The compiler's own disassembler already carried the full trading range, with a
comment about having been fixed for exactly this reason. The lesson is not new
here, but it keeps recurring: this format has four readers and every one of them
has to be updated together.

`x3c run` over `examples/*.x3`, end to end through build → verify → execute:

```

```

The trading core is the centrepiece of the economic-execution thesis and its
bytecode could not be validated or executed at all before this.

Remaining runtime failures, filed as TICKET-025: `examples/mainnet_safe_swap.x3`
and `examples/flagship_b52.x3` — the two examples the repo uses to demonstrate a
mainnet-safe cross-chain swap — build and verify, then panic with
`solver bid: fee must be non-empty`.
